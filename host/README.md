# Tasktap (host)

The rebuilt Tasktap that can be tried: the Mac host (Tauri v2) with the notch and the workspace window, running on the new core in `core/`. Its pages are in `ui/`. The same host builds for Windows, where a tab at the top of the screen and an icon in the notification area stand in for the notch: see "Windows" below.

## Build and run

    host/scripts/build.sh --open

It builds the pages, the core and the host, and opens `host/target/release/bundle/macos/Tasktap.app`. Needs Rust, Node 22 and macOS 13 or later. Quit an installed Tasktap first: its notch sits in the same place and uses the same shortcuts.

The app's identity is `io.github.harzerheribert.tasktap`, and it keeps everything in `~/Library/Application Support/Tasktap` (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\Tasktap` on Windows): the core's database, the core's copy of itself in `bin/<build>/`, the host's device key, and `host.json` (the workspace window's frame). A build run from here is the same app with the same folder; the checks give theirs a temporary one (`TASKTAP_DATA`).

Tasktap is in the Dock and has a menu. Closing the workspace window leaves the notch; the Dock icon brings the workspace back. Quit it with ⌘Q. The core keeps running a minute after the last window is gone, or as long as a terminal runs: quitting never stops a session.

### A review copy beside it

    host/scripts/build-review.sh [--open]

builds `host/target/review/release/bundle/macos/Tasktap Review.app`: the same app, with the host's `review` feature and `tauri.review.conf.json` over `tauri.conf.json`, to try a change while Tasktap runs. Its identity is `io.github.harzerheribert.tasktap.review`, and everything of it is in `~/Library/Application Support/Tasktap Review` (Windows: `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Tasktap Review`): its own core and database, socket or pipe, copy of the core and device key. It has no notch (on Windows no tab until a display is chosen in its Settings); it takes no global key, the capture key and the keys that answer a question alike, recorded or not (they are the person's own Tasktap's), and its Settings and title bar name none; closing its window leaves it in the Dock. It never updates itself and never takes over another folder. Its core keeps a speech service's key in the Keychain under a service of its own, `Tasktap Review` (`TASKTAP_KEY_SERVICE`, `core/src/secrets.rs`), so the two apps' keys never meet.

## What works

- **The notch.** Glance, capture with its destination chip (your pick, else where the core's router would send the words, else the default project), Undo, the answer card, results. See the notch section of `docs/rebuild-plan.md`. Its Open shows the session in the workspace.
- **Placing by a model.** Once the person has accepted the sheet that shows what is sent and to whom, a thought that names no folder or session is placed, and its brief written, by the smallest model of their own Claude Code, Codex or Antigravity (`core/src/route.rs`, `ui/src/workspace/consent.tsx`; design §8). Without that consent a thought goes where it names, by the core's own router.
- **The workspace window**, in Instrument (`design/ui-directions/NOTES.md`; tokens in `ui/src/tokens.css`):
  - Panes with tabs: New terminal (⌘T, or ＋ at the end of the tabs), Split right (⌘D) and Split down (⇧⌘D), nested in both directions; drag a divider to resize, double-click to balance; drag a tab to another pane or to a pane's edge; the focused pane's tab lit, the others' terminals stepped back. No per-pane toolbar: a tab's right-click opens the system's menu with Find, Copy, Paste, text size, Move tab to…, Keep running in background, Stop and Close; the window's menu and keys do the same.
  - Terminals: xterm.js on the core's streams, in one look, Default: Instrument's black, a GitHub-dark palette, Hack 14 with the Nerd Fonts symbols, both shipped with the app, set at the nearest size whose cells are a whole number of pixels wide (so blocks and box lines join) and with every colour kept legible on what it is drawn over; Settings › Terminal sets another installed family, the size and the line height. ⌘C and ⌘V (bracketed paste, no Enter added); ⌘+, ⌘−, ⌘0 per terminal, remembered in the layout; Find in terminal (⌘F); Release mouse capture. Links: a web address, a path that exists (from the terminal's folder; with `:line` and `:line:column` as compilers print them) and a hyperlink a program prints itself are links while ⌘ is held: underlined under the pointer, ⌘-click opens (a web address in the browser, a file of text in the default text editor), ⇧⌘-click shows a file in Finder; where a link's text is not its target, the target stands at the terminal's foot. A plain click is always the program's.
  - A question shows in a strip under its terminal, stated once with its answers as key caps (1 to 9, T to reply, while the strip has the keyboard). A run that worked in its own copy of the project shows its result there with Keep (↩), Discard and Review changes.
  - Every interruption is a sheet hanging from the title bar: closing a live tab (Keep running in background, Stop session, Cancel), stopping a session, closing a workspace, opening a folder, Find, moving a tab, New task (⌘N), a routing question, Keep and Discard and their review, a session's details (⌘I: its agent and state, the folder or the copy it runs in, and each turn as the thought that began it with what was asked and what came back), Agents (where the default agent is chosen), Settings (⌘,).
  - The sidebar: Home, then each open workspace with its sessions, most urgent first, each with its state mark; a session sent to the background is listed there dimmed, and a click shows its terminal again, continued where it was, with nothing started. ＋ opens a folder or a closed workspace again.
  - Home, in layers: the capture line, what needs you (the first question open in full), changes waiting to be kept, what is running; Results and Ideas as lines that open in place; All results leads to Earlier. The rail at the window's foot says the same in words and opens Agents and Settings.
  - The arrangement is the project's `layouts` record; a terminal it does not mention (a run the core started) gets a place in the selected pane.
  - Previews beside a terminal (`design/ui-directions/NOTES.md`, section 12): ⌘-click on a link shows a web address of this machine, a picture, a PDF, a page or Markdown in a pane at the terminal's right, a tab for each (⌥⌘-click opens it elsewhere instead, ⇧⌘-click shows a file in Finder); an agent shows what it made with `tasktap preview <file or address>`, without taking the keyboard. ⌘P moves between a terminal and its preview, ⌘R reloads it, ⌘W closes it. A page, a PDF and a local address are shown in a frame the host holds to this machine (`src/frames.rs`): it reaches nothing of the app, loads nothing from the network, and is stopped where it tries to leave, with the address offered for the browser. On Windows frames are not held yet, so a page there is offered to the browser or the editor.
  - Settings: what Return does in a thought, the capture key and the hold-to-talk key (recorded; refused in a sentence where the system, another app or the rule of a shortcut says no), how long finished thoughts are kept, the terminal's two colour switches, and text size.
- **The light.** Home's light is a few soft pools of the person's motion colour and one low band of the motion's chrome, drawn by `ui/src/motion/`; the sidebar's foot has the same light with a few large chrome glyphs, each a picture drawn once, there only where the rows leave room. At rest all of it is still and costs nothing. While something goes on in the window in use (agents thinking or working, a question waiting) it is a loop for as long as that lasts: the band drifts and pulses (the page draws keyframes of it and the compositor carries it between them, so the page is given no frames while the loop lasts), the pools breathe and the glyphs sway, slowly, on one clock (`--t-ambient`); a change of state blends into the next, a result or a region taking focus is a moment over it. The sidebar's edge and a dropping sheet shed a short wake of the same glyphs. Settings › Appearance chooses the form, the colour or a hue, Lumen, and Effects (Off, Still, Full: the one control over everything that moves by itself; Reduce Motion and Low Power Mode hold Full at Still), all the core's preferences, as are the terminal's font, size and line height; it can try the motion with the microphone while the sheet is open: macOS asks once, with the reason in `Info.plist`. The notch's sheet carries no form; its glow takes Lumen's colours.
- **Speech on this Mac.** Settings › Speech › On this Mac downloads a model once (Whisper large-v3 turbo, 574 MB, from huggingface.co, checked against its checksum, kept in the data folder's `models/`, removable there). After that, with no speech service chosen, what you say while you hold ⌥Space or the microphone is turned into text on this Mac and sent nowhere: the words appear a sentence or two behind while you speak, and the rest well under a second after you let go. Nothing of it runs while you are not speaking. See design §8, "On this device".
- **Keyboard.** A focus move by script never shows a focus ring.

## Windows

    pwsh host\scripts\build.ps1 [-Target x86_64|aarch64]

It builds the pages, the core and the host and puts what runs into one folder, `host\target\<target>-pc-windows-msvc\release\Tasktap\`, and the same folder into a zip beside it: `tasktap-app.exe` (not `Tasktap.exe`, which Windows would not tell from the core's name), the core (`tasktap.exe`), Microsoft's console (`conpty.dll`, `OpenConsole.exe`: the core's terminals run in it, `core/scripts/conpty.ps1`) and the licences. Needs Rust with the target, Visual Studio Build Tools and Node; to run, the WebView2 runtime that Windows 11 has. The app keeps everything in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Tasktap`. `-Installer` also makes the installer (below).

Built, and checked as far as a machine without a signed-in desktop allows (`scripts/winvm/host.sh`, `host\scripts\smoke.ps1`): the host compiles and links for x64 and ARM64 with Clippy clean; it starts the core and talks to it over the core's named pipe, each page through its own connection (`src/windows.rs`); the workspace window, dark as a whole, whose title bar is the page's own top row with a menu button and the caption buttons in it (`src/chrome.rs`), and whose commands have Windows' own keys (`ui/src/keys.ts`; the plan's "Keys on Windows"). The system's folder dialog, modal to the window (`host.pick`); previews beside a terminal, with WebView2 held to this machine's addresses as the Mac's web view is (`src/frames.rs`, `src/windows.rs`); a web address, a file of text and a place opened by Windows' own programs (`src/open.rs`); pictures of a page (`snapshot`). At a desktop (`scripts/winvm/desktop.sh`, on the tower): the window in under half a second, the capture key, a terminal with `cmd.exe` and PowerShell, Control-C, the credential store, the window's buttons, edges, menus and Snap layouts tried with the pointer, the folder dialog opened, cancelled and chosen in, and a thought sent from another program with the fixture agent answering. Built since (2026-11-19): the tab that stands for the notch and the icon in the notification area (`src/edge.rs`, `src/tray.rs`, `src/tab.rs`; the plan's "The notch on Windows", "Built", says what was tried and what was not); closing the window leaves the app in the notification area, and Settings, Thoughts chooses the tab's display or turns it off. Not built: being told when battery saver begins or ends. `scripts/winvm/README.md`, "At the desktop", lists what a person has to look at.

Nothing for Windows is code-signed, by choice: a downloaded copy is stopped by SmartScreen until the person chooses to run it ("Release" below). Microsoft's two files keep Microsoft's signature.

## Release

`.github/workflows/release.yml`, from a tag `v<x.y.z>`: the Mac app for Apple silicon, sealed and notarised, as a disk image and as the updater's pack; Windows x64 as an installer (Tauri's NSIS, for the person alone, so no administrator is asked and the updater can replace it) and as the zip above, neither code-signed; the updater's feed (`latest.json`); all of it as a draft GitHub Release, which the owner publishes. Run by hand, or by a push to `release-dry-run`, it is a dry run: signed ad hoc, not notarised, nothing published.

- **Sealing** (`scripts/sign-mac.sh`, `scripts/check-signed.sh`): every program in the bundle signed with the hardened runtime from the inside out; the app with the microphone and Apple Events (`entitlements/app.plist` says why), the core and the speech helper with nothing. Tauri's own signing gives every program the same entitlements, so it is not used. The check lists every program in the bundle and fails on one unsigned, unhardened, with other entitlements or unknown. On a person's Mac only ad hoc: `host/scripts/sign-mac.sh <app>`.
- **Notarising and the disk image** (`scripts/release-mac.sh`): the app is notarised and stapled, the disk image made from it, signed, notarised and stapled, and the stapled app packed for the updater and signed with the updater's key.
- **Opening at login** (`src/login.rs`, Settings › Thoughts): the system's own item, read back from the system and never stored. On the Mac `SMAppService.mainAppService`, listed in System Settings › General › Login Items (only a bundled, signed copy; any other says so in its row); on Windows the person's Run entry with `--login` for the installed app, no administrator, listed in Startup apps. A review build and a check's run never ask the system. Started at login the app opens no window, only the notch or the tab and the icon. The checks test the decision and the call that would be made; none registers anything.
- **The updater** (`src/update.rs`, `ui/src/workspace/update.tsx`): Tauri's, with a key pair of its own; its public key is `plugins.updater.pubkey` in `tauri.conf.json`, its private key only in the repository's secrets. Only the release workflow's builds look (`TASKTAP_UPDATES`): when Settings › About is shown, and at the first capture of a day, while About's Check for new versions is on (the `update_checks` preference, on by default); off, only when the person presses Check now there (`host.update {look, now}`); never at start, never on a timer. A new version is said in About and in one quiet line in the rail, and installed only from About: on the Mac in place, then the app restarts; on Windows the installer runs and opens the app again. The core and its sessions run on either way.
- **The install lines** (`site/install.sh`, `site/install.ps1`; `scripts/install-check.sh`, `scripts/install-check.ps1`): each release also carries `Tasktap_aarch64.dmg` and `Tasktap_x64-setup.exe`, copies of the disk image and the installer by names without the version, and `SHA256SUMS`, the SHA-256 of every file of the release (a dry run makes and checks them too). `curl -fsSL https://harzerheribert.github.io/Tasktap/install.sh | sh` and `irm https://harzerheribert.github.io/Tasktap/install.ps1 | iex` take those copies through `releases/latest/download/`, check them against `SHA256SUMS` (the Mac's also Apple's notarisation and the team in its signature, which is `TEAM_ID` in `install.sh` and installs nothing while empty) and install for the person alone, never as an administrator; on Windows that avoids SmartScreen's stop, since a file a script fetched carries no mark of the internet. The checks try every refusal with stand-ins, and a signed run of the release workflow tries the Mac's accepting path on the image it has just notarised.
- **The takeover** (`src/takeover.rs`, `scripts/takeover-check.sh`): builds were called "Tasktap Next", with their own identity and data folder. At its first start Tasktap moves that folder to its own (`tasktap move-data`, after a copy of the database, with the records' paths and the projects' copies following it), once, and only when nothing of Tasktap Next runs; otherwise it says what to quit and does not start. Nothing else of Tasktap Next is supported.

## How it fits together

- `src/core.rs`: the data folder, the copy of the core, the device key, start on demand with `start.lock` as `core/src/client.rs` does it, and the host's own connection (it watches `glance` and sends `input`).
- `src/main.rs`: the bridge of design §12. Each page gets its own core connection, said hello to by the host with its device key; after that the bytes pass through untouched both ways (`bridge_open`, `bridge_send`) and `host.*` calls are answered here (`bridge_host`): `host.notch`, `host.show`, `host.ready`, `host.pick`, `host.clipboard`, `host.open`, `host.capture_key` and `host.where`. Also the global keys, sent where `src/presence.rs` says, `host.notify` from the core (`src/notify.rs`), and the debug channel.
- `src/presence.rs`: where Tasktap shows itself (plan, "Where Tasktap shows itself"): the rule, a pure function of which app is in front, what the window shows, the waiting question, full screen and the system, tested on both systems; "the window in front" decided once, from the system's own notices, and told to both pages at once.
- `src/keys.rs`: the capture key. It is the core's `capture_key` preference, registered when the records arrive and whenever they change; `host.capture_key` lets Settings change it and hear at once when the system refuses a key, which leaves the old one working.
- `src/open.rs`: `host.open` and its rule, which is the host's and not the page's: a web address (http or https), or something that exists inside a folder the person opened in Tasktap or a copy Tasktap made of one, with every link on the way followed first. A file of text opens in the default text editor; anything else, any folder, and anything on request is shown in Finder. Nothing is ever run, and everything else is refused in a sentence. The host knows the folders from the core (`projects`, on its own connection).
- `src/workspace.rs`: the workspace window, its frame, the application menu with the About panel's notices, and Dock reopening.
- `src/speech.rs`, `speech/engine.cpp`: speech on this Mac (on Windows the host says it is not available, and voice needs a speech service). The page records as it does for a speech service and hands the samples to the host, which starts `tasktap-speech` (whisper.cpp, built by `scripts/build-speech.sh`; needs cmake, or python3 to fetch one) for that one dictation and passes its lines back; and the model's download, check and removal.
- `src/notch.rs`, `src/native.rs`, `src/geometry.rs`: the notch panel from `trials/tauri-notch`, with pointer events in place of the 80 ms poll; the folder panel and the clipboard.
- `src/windows.rs`, `src/tab.rs`: on Windows `windows.rs` is compiled in place of `native.rs`, under the same names: the core's pipe, the clipboard, the power state, the folder dialog, what a preview's frame is held to by WebView2's own means, a page's picture, and "there is no panel" for what the notch asks; `tab.rs` is where the tab will sit.
- `../ui/`: the pages (`npm run check` type-checks them and tests the shared client and the layout model).

## Checks

    cd ui && npm run check
    cd host && cargo clippy -- -D warnings && cargo fmt --check && cargo test
    host/scripts/smoke.sh        # the notch and the workspace with the core's fixture agent; PASS or FAIL lines
    SPEECH_MODEL=<ggml-large-v3-turbo-q5_0.bin> host/scripts/smoke.sh   # the same, and speech on this Mac with the real model, from a file you fetched
    host/scripts/smoke.sh --only Settings   # the app's start and the sections whose names hold these words (comma between several); no headless checks: a step that fails now and then, in a loop
    node scripts/speech-check.cjs && node scripts/speech-app-check.cjs   # speech on this Mac alone: the page (headless), then the app
    node scripts/placing-check.cjs <folder>   # the Placing sheet and its group in Settings by the keyboard (headless, fixture records), pictures in the folder
    host/scripts/measure.sh      # idle cost with the workspace on one idle terminal, launch, typing delay, a burst; after build.sh
    MOTION=1 host/scripts/measure.sh   # what the effects cost, per process: at rest behind and in front, agents working and a question waiting on Home (carried, and drawn by the page), beside a terminal, each level's memory
    FLOW=1 host/scripts/measure.sh     # the capture flow, timed: frames and keys while a thought is typed, sent, placed, worked on and answered, at each level
    CADENCE=1 host/scripts/measure.sh  # the band frame by frame: its cadence, its steps and its clocks through a capture and over its pulses
    node scripts/subpixel-check.cjs    # the forms move in hundredths of a pixel (headless; --app for the app's own web view)
    node scripts/carry-check.cjs       # what the compositor carries is the picture the page would draw in every frame (headless, on the graphics card)
    node scripts/measure-all.cjs <folder> --front   # all four in the built app in one run, on every display the Mac has, kept in the folder
    host/scripts/gpu.sh          # what the graphics processor costs, state by state; takes over the screen for about 15 minutes (below)

`smoke.sh` and `measure.sh` drive the app only through its debug channel (`TASKTAP_DEBUG=1`: one command per line on standard input) and run it passive (`TASKTAP_PASSIVE=1`: a passive run is never given the microphone, whatever a check asks for, so voice in a check is a tone or a recording handed to the page; no window ever takes the keyboard, the app stays out of the Dock and the menu bar, the person's pointer is not watched, and Copy and Paste use a pasteboard of their own), so they are safe to run while you work. The three measures above say which display their window is on and its refresh rate, put the window where the fewest others are (`DISPLAY_HZ=144` picks the display), and refuse a sleeping, locked or covered screen, on which nothing is drawn. A passive window opens behind the app in use; `--front` on any of the node scripts (the debug channel's `front`) puts it in front of the other apps' windows for the run, without taking the keyboard. A passive run is never put in front by the system either, so a check says where the window is as the system would (`where front|behind|system`, `where full on|off`), presses a key from any app as the system tells of it (`hotkey capture|j|y|n`) and clicks elsewhere (`elsewhere`); `where` alone logs where Tasktap is. `snapshot <page> <file>` on that channel writes a picture of a page as the app itself draws it, with no screen recording permission involved (`SHOTS=<folder> host/scripts/smoke.sh`).

## What the graphics processor costs

`host/scripts/gpu.sh` measures what Tasktap costs this Mac's graphics processor, state by state, as `scripts/winvm/gpu.sh` does on Windows. Unlike the checks above it runs the app as a person does (not passive: a passive app's light rests whatever it shows) and **takes over the screen for about a quarter of an hour**: Tasktap's window comes in front and stays there, the notch opens and glows. Nobody may touch the Mac meanwhile; the round says so when it starts and says when it is done.

```sh
host/scripts/gpu.sh --print-powermetrics   # 1. the command to start first, in a second Terminal window, with your password
host/scripts/gpu.sh                        # 2. the round: every state for a minute at the window's size, effects Full (about 15 minutes)
host/scripts/gpu.sh --effects still,off --seconds 30
host/scripts/gpu.sh --size full            # the window as large as the display less its menu bar and Dock
host/scripts/gpu.sh --rest                 # the proof alone: the floor, Home at rest with and without the caret, the notch at rest
host/scripts/gpu.sh --app <other>/Tasktap.app   # another build, to compare (its core with the fixture agent beside it, or --core)
host/scripts/gpu.sh --dry-run              # starts nothing: could a round start now, the floor for five seconds, what it would measure
host/scripts/gpu.sh --rehearse             # every state for five seconds on a Mac in use: the round's mechanics, no figures
```

**What the owner does.** Restart the Mac and leave everything closed (Tasktap too: another Tasktap's notch would lie over this one's), keep it plugged in with Low Power Mode off, and put the pointer in a corner of the screen. In a second Terminal window start the command `--print-powermetrics` prints (with the same options as the round, so that it lasts long enough) and type the password there; then start the round in the first window and leave the Mac alone until it says it is done. Without that log the round reports only what it reads without a password, and says so.

**What it refuses.** A locked screen or a sleeping display (nothing drawn there can be measured), the Mac on battery, in Low Power Mode (the effects rest there) or hot, another Tasktap running, the pointer where the window or the notch will be; and after its floor (a minute with no app): a 1-minute load average over 4 (a quiet Mac sits under 2 once the indexing after a restart is done), another process using more than 5 ms of the graphics processor a second, the graphics processor more than 5 % busy, or WindowServer drawing more than 20 ms a second with nothing to draw. `--force` measures anyway over the floor's and the pointer's refusals.

**The states** are Windows' with the notch for the tab: `rest` (Home, nothing running, the caret in its field), `rest-unfocused` (the caret out of it), `notch-rest` and `notch-open` (the window closed, the notch folded, then open as the capture key opens it), `sidebar` (one slide a second), `terminal` (zsh printing lines as fast as it can), `listening` (the level of the checks' tone put where the core's report of a recording goes: the microphone is taken from both pages before anything else, and nothing opens it), `thinking` (a thought held on its way), `working` (the fixture agent in its turn), `question` (its question waiting, on Home) and `notch-question` (the window closed, the notch glowing for it). The window is brought in front with the app's own `show`, which makes it the active app's key window as a click does; where macOS does not let the app become active the round says so and uses `front` (no caret blinks then). Each state is settled (ten seconds at rest: focus sends a pulse of 7.2 s) and measured for `--seconds` at each level of `--effects`; a floor with no app comes first and again, half a minute, at the end. `--size full` is the window given the display's visible frame (as Fill makes it), not the green button's full screen: that moves the window to a Space of its own under the camera housing, where the notch shows only when opened (rule 6), which changes what the notch's states measure; and a frame in the app's temporary data folder works for any build `--app` names. The notch's three states are left out there.

**What it reads, each second.** Without a password (`scripts/gpu-sample.swift`): the graphics processor's utilisation from its own statistics (`ioreg -r -d 1 -c IOAccelerator`, on this Mac `AGXAcceleratorG16X`: `PerformanceStatistics` › `Device Utilization %`, and `Renderer` and `Tiler Utilization %` in samples.csv); each process's graphics time from the driver's count of its clients (the `AGXDeviceUserClient` entries under the accelerator: `IOUserClientCreator` names the process, `AppUsage` › `accumulatedGPUTime` counts ns), summed for the app's processes, for WindowServer and for the busiest other process; the app's processes' processor time; the frames the page's light drew (`__motion.stats.frames`); the display's refresh rate; where the window and the notch are and whether another app's window lies over them; and the HID system's idle time, so that a person using the Mac is noted. The app's processes are the app, what runs from its temporary folder (its core) and everything they start, and its WebKit content, GPU and networking processes (an app started from a terminal is not responsible for itself: they are the WebKit processes its terminal became responsible for after the app began). With the owner's log (`sudo powermetrics --samplers gpu_power,tasks --show-process-gpu -i 1000`, into the rounds' folder) also the graphics processor's power and each process's graphics time as powermetrics gives them, read by the samples' time stamps.

**What the figures mean.** `table.txt` has a row per state and level: the graphics processor's utilisation over the floor, its power over the floor (mW), the app's processes' graphics time (ms a second: the driver's count, mean and busiest second, and powermetrics'), WindowServer's (it composes every window, and redraws the window's material whenever the page under it changes), the page's frames a second, the display's rate and the app's processor time (% of one core). The app's graphics time is its own whatever else runs; utilisation and power are the whole Mac's and mean something only on a quiet one. `samples.csv` has every second, `results.txt` what the round said, `app.txt` what the app said; all in `~/Library/Logs/tasktap-gpu-rounds/<time>` (`--out`), never in the repository.

**Zero at rest** (item 35 of `docs/trial-checklist.md`). `rest-unfocused` and `notch-rest` PASS only when in every second the page drew no frame and the app's processes had no graphics time at all (the driver's count, and powermetrics' too when its log is there); `rest`, with the caret in Home's field, at most the caret's blink: no frame, and no second over 0.5 ms of graphics time (a blink redraws a few points twice a second; a light that draws at all draws 120 times a second). A second in which the window or the notch was not seen fails the state. `--rest` runs the floor and these three and fails the command otherwise.

`--rehearse` runs every state for five seconds, the floors for five and three, on a Mac in use: what would refuse a round is said, not obeyed, and no verdict is given. `host/scripts/gpu.sh --self-test` reads `host/scripts/powermetrics-fixture.txt`, written from the format strings in `/usr/bin/powermetrics` (which will not run without root): the real log's format is confirmed only by the first round that has one.
