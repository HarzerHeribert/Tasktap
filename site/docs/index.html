How it works

A short guide to Tasktap as it is built today. The app is in development; what is missing from this page is listed at its end.

Capture

Press ⌃⌥Space in any app. The notch opens on one line: type the thought and press ↩. Or hold ⌥Space in that line and talk, and let go to send it.

The window’s Home starts with the same field, and ⌘N opens it from a workspace. The field says where the thought will go, and its key says what ↩ will do: Start, or Save for later. A thought saved for later waits on Home until you send it to a folder.

Placing and the brief

At first a thought goes to the folder or the session it names. You can let a model place the thoughts that name nothing. It is the smallest model of your own Claude Code, Codex or Antigravity, and the sheet that asks shows who reads your thoughts, what is sent with each one, and the instructions the model follows. ↩ allows it, esc declines, and Settings › Projects, at Finding the project for a thought, turns it off again. Once allowed, a long thought that names its project is read too, for its summary.

A thought is placed among the folders you have opened in Tasktap. A map of the projects on your Mac, so that a thought also finds one you have not opened yet, is designed and being built.

The same model writes the brief, which is what the agent receives. It states the goal, the limits you gave, and what counts as done if you said it. It adds nothing you did not say. Your own words always travel with it. A short, clear thought goes on as it is.

Once placed, the thought waits under the field for a few seconds: five, unless you change Wait before an agent starts a thought in Settings › Thoughts, and longer while its project is still being found. You see where it goes, its brief, the words you said, and Undo on ⌘Z. The lit line above it runs out with the time that is left. ↩ on the empty field starts it at once.

If the model cannot tell where a thought belongs, it asks you: “Where should this go?”, with the folders and sessions it could mean on keys, and L to keep it for later.

Questions

While agents work, the folded notch shows counts: working, needing you, finished. When an agent needs an answer, the notch opens by itself and shows the question in full.

Choices are on 1 to 9. A yes or no is also on Y and N. T opens a reply in your own words. With several waiting the notch says which one you see, “2 of 3”, and → shows the next.

The same question stands first on Home and under its terminal in the workspace, so you answer it wherever you are. Some questions can only be answered in the agent’s own terminal; the card then leads there. Which agent asks where is listed under Agents.

Results, Keep and Discard

A finished thought comes back as a result: a summary, and its changes when it ran in its own copy of the project.

In a git project that already has work running, a new thought gets its own copy (a git worktree) and its own branch, so two runs cannot overwrite each other. Settings › Projects, in a project’s details at Where its agent works, can give every thought its own copy, or none. Keep adds its commits to your branch by a fast-forward, the same one you would run yourself; if your branch has moved on meanwhile, by a merge commit, and if the two conflict Keep changes nothing and names the files. Discard removes the copy. Review changes shows the diff first. In the notch they are on ↩, D and, to open the session, O.

Tasktap never deletes or rewrites anything outside its own folder and its own branches. If something unexpected happens it stops and reports the real state. Folders without git run in place.

The workspace

One window. The sidebar lists Home and every open folder as a workspace, with its sessions and the state of each. A workspace holds real terminals in panes with tabs.

Drag a divider to resize panes and a tab to move it to another pane. A tab’s right-click menu has the rest, including Keep running in background: the session leaves the panes, stays in the sidebar, and a click brings its terminal back where it left off.

A link in a terminal is one while ⌘ is held. ⌘-click on an address of this Mac, a picture, a PDF, a page or Markdown shows it in a preview at the terminal’s right, and an agent can show what it made there with tasktap preview. A preview reaches nothing of the app and loads nothing from the network.

Quitting the app does not stop a session. The core keeps running for as long as a terminal runs.

Agents

Tasktap starts ten agents: Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity, OpenCode, Pi, Qwen Code, Goose, Crush, Droid and Aider. You install each and sign in to it yourself, on your own subscription; Settings › Agents shows which are installed and signed in, and which one runs new thoughts. Any other agent that runs from a command line can be added there by its command, such as goose run -t {prompt}.

Claude Code, OpenCode
Questions, and requests to run a command or change a file, are answered from the notch, Home or the workspace.
Codex
Its requests are answered there too; its questions in its terminal.
Antigravity, Pi, Qwen Code
What they ask is shown, and answered in their terminal.
Goose, Crush, Droid, Aider
They run one thought to its end and ask nothing on the way. A follow-up waits for that end. Aider ends the same way whether it did the work or not; its terminal says which.

Tasktap never writes to an agent’s own settings: what it needs for a run is given to that run, from Tasktap’s own folder.

Voice

Hold ⌥Space in the capture field or in a reply, or press and hold the microphone. The key works while Tasktap is in front; in an empty field, letting go sends the thought, and E before letting go keeps the words to edit. A short click on the microphone keeps it on until the next click. While it records, the light on the mark follows your voice.

Speech can be turned into text on the Mac itself. Settings › Voice, at Where your speech becomes text, downloads the model once: Whisper large-v3 turbo, 574 MB, checked before it is used. After that what you say is written while you speak, a sentence or two behind, and sent nowhere; nothing of it runs while you are not speaking. It was tried in German and English, and in both mixed in one dictation, as a developer speaks them.

Or speech is turned into text by a service you choose: OpenAI, Groq or another server, with your own key. Before anything is stored, the sheet shows exactly what is sent and where. A recording is at most five minutes. You can name the languages you speak, as a hint.

On Windows

Windows is being built, and can be built from source. Windows has no notch: a small tab hangs from the top edge of the screen and opens into the same sheet, and an icon by the clock says what waits and opens it too. The tab hides while a full-screen app or a presentation runs. Settings › Thoughts, at Display that shows Tasktap’s tab, chooses its display, or None turns it off.

Keys follow one rule: Ctrl stands where the Mac has ⌘, and with a letter Shift is held too, so Ctrl and a letter alone stay the terminal’s. New terminal is Ctrl+Shift+T, and Find Ctrl+Shift+F. The capture key is Alt+Shift+Space; hold to talk is Ctrl+Space.

So far Claude Code, Codex and the agents you add run on Windows. Speech there goes through a speech service. Nothing is signed yet, so Windows asks before a copy built elsewhere runs.

Settings

⌘, opens Settings, its sections at its left in this order, on ⌘1 to ⌘7. Each row says what you decide, and one sentence under it what changes for you.

Thoughts
Key to put a thought in, from any app. On Windows, the display that shows Tasktap’s tab. Return key when writing a thought: sends the thought, or starts a new line. Wait before an agent starts a thought: no wait, 3, 5 or 10 seconds, at least, since finding the project can take longer; ⌘Z takes it back meanwhile. Delete finished thoughts after: never, 30 days, 90 days or a year, with their answers and the terminal’s history.
Agents
Which agents are installed and signed in, which one runs new thoughts, what an agent may do only once you let it, and the ones you add. Check which agents are signed in.
Projects
Finding the project for a thought: only thoughts that name their project, or your agents in the order you set, with one agreement for all of them. Where Tasktap finds your projects, and every project it knows; opened, where its agent works: on its own copy when another agent is working there, always on its own copy, or in the project itself, where two agents at once can overwrite each other’s changes. A folder without Git always runs in itself.
Voice
Where your speech becomes text: on the Mac, or at a speech service with your key. The languages you speak. Key to hold while you talk.
Appearance
Text size. Effects, the form of the chrome (Slats, Glyphs or Resonator), its colour, and Lumen, the coloured light behind it.
Terminal
Font, text size and space between lines in terminals. Bold text in brighter colours, and text that is hard to read adjusted.
About
The version, and Check for new versions: on, Tasktap asks GitHub when About opens and at the first thought of a day; off, it asks only when you press Check now. A new version is installed only when you say. What Tasktap keeps and what it sends, the check for a new version at GitHub among it while it is on, and the licences of what it ships with.

Effects

Home has soft light and a band of chrome, and the foot of the sidebar a few chrome glyphs. At rest they are still and cost nothing. They move only while something goes on: agents working, a question waiting, your voice.

One setting covers all of it: Full, Still, which keeps the picture and stops the motion, or Off, which draws none of it. While Reduce Motion or Low Power Mode is on, Full behaves as Still.
